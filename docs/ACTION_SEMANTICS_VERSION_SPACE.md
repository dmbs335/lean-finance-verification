# Action-Semantics Version Spaces

One observed attack trace rarely identifies one unique workflow semantics. A guard inferred from the observed pre-state may be only one member of a larger family of guards that replay the same trace. Selecting one convenient member can therefore underestimate the evidence required by every semantics still consistent with the observations.

## Formal criterion

`VersionSpace consistent traces model` means that `model` is consistent with every observed trace. `ModelFamilyVerifies` requires the claim to remain constant on equal evidence across all worlds generated by all surviving models.

The formal layer proves:

- adding traces can only shrink a version space;
- family-wide verification implies verification after restriction to any point model;
- a cross-model indistinguishable counterexample refutes family verification;
- a proof-carrying `PointUnderestimationWitness` can establish that one chosen model verifies while the complete version space does not.

## Exact finite synthesizer

`tools/model_family_synth` accepts a finite set of candidate semantics, their worlds, claims, evidence-channel observations, and costs. It computes two exact optima:

1. the point optimum after selecting one model;
2. the family optimum across every model still consistent with the traces.

The checked-in guard-ambiguity fixture has two semantics that both replay the observed cost-model mutation trace. Publication evidence costs 1 and verifies the narrow point model. Under the permissive semantics, however, a silent mutation has the same publication observation as an honest execution. The complete version space therefore requires publication evidence plus an independent mutation receipt, with total cost 4 and an underestimation gap of 3.

This is a bounded result. The candidate semantics and worlds must be generated by a sound hypothesis language and workflow explorer. The next layer should synthesize Boolean guard/effect hypotheses from positive and negative traces rather than receiving the family as a hand-written finite input.
